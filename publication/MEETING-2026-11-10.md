## 4. Disk utilisation, and the limit it sets

[v3-ssd results](data/v3/v3-ssd/), [device.json](data/v3/v3-ssd/device.json)

You suspected a hidden I/O bottleneck twice. On a SATA SSD with direct I/O, both designs
saturate the device at 100% utilisation and 220 to 244 MiB/s, so throughput converges.

| Cores | Throughput | CPU per write | p99.9 |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 8 | 0.496 to 0.462 M/s (0.93x) | 6.55 to 3.50 µs (**1.87x less**) | 48.2 to 28.3 ms |
| 16 | 0.497 to 0.516 M/s (1.04x) | 6.84 to 3.43 µs (**1.99x less**) | 335.5 to 83.9 ms |

**Read this as a scoping result, not a loss.** When the disk is the wall you get the same
throughput for half the CPU and a 1.7x to 4.0x better tail. The throughput claim is a
CPU-bound-regime claim, and this SATA SSD caps at roughly 225 MiB/s, about 20x slower than
NVMe. The untested regime that matters is NVMe, and that is the next experiment.

## 6. Prior designs, rebuilt and measured

[fig22_prior_gpu_designs.png](figures/fig22_prior_gpu_designs.png), [RELATED-WORK.md](RELATED-WORK.md)

gLSM/gParaKV-style and LUDA/FPGA-style offload were reimplemented inside the same RocksDB
and measured on the same harness, under preregistered fairness rules that favour them.

| Workload | Best earlier | Ours | Gap |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 128 B ZSTD, CPU s/GiB | 14.15 | 2.19 | 6.5x |
| 128 B LZ4, CPU s/GiB | 5.44 | 2.10 | 2.6x |
| Whole DB, ZSTD | 246 kops/s | 475 | 1.93x |
| Whole DB, no compression | 576 kops/s | 586 | **none** |

**Without compression the best earlier design matches ours.** The contribution is moving
compression and its checksums to the GPU, which is most of what earlier designs leave on the
CPU, and it is worth nothing if a deployment turns compression off.

gParaKV run unmodified under its own driver beats us: 238 against 182 kops/s at 128 B values.
It separates values out of the sorted files, so it moves far fewer bytes through compaction.
We cut its CPU by 36% instead. The two approaches are complementary.

## 7. RDMA

[fig23_rdma_gpu.png](figures/fig23_rdma_gpu.png), [r7 summary](data/rdma/r7/summary.md)

| Design | Time | Database-node CPU |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Native RocksDB | 5.28 s | 8.17 s/GiB |
| GPU in the database process | 0.86 s | 1.21 s/GiB |
| Remote GPU over RDMA plus relay | 11.88 s | 1.28 s/GiB |

The CPU offload is real and measured: 6.4x off the database node. The wall-clock result is a
loss, and the cause is diagnosed rather than guessed. The GPU machine is not on the RoCE
fabric, so 10.57 s of that 11.88 s is one 1 GbE hop. RDMA and TCP land within 2% of each
other because neither transport is the bottleneck, so H13 and H14 cannot be evaluated on this
hardware. Fixing it is a topology change, not a design change.

No latency estimate for an RDMA-attached GPU is reported as a result.
